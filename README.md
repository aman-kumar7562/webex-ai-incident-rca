🚀 From Chasing Alerts to Asking Questions: How AI Changed Our Incident Response, RCA, and Recovery 🚀
Wait! Have we moved beyond static and traditional dashboards. The answer is yes we finally pressed the accelerator on innovation and glided into the AI era - where investigations became an adventure driven by intelligence, context, and speed, all inside our IDE and that's the magic of Model Context Protocol (MCP).
🧠 Life Before AI: The Traditional Way
Some time back, incident investigation meant jumping between tools - again and again.
 We manually scrubbed dashboards for metrics, searched logs for clues, checked network signals in ThousandEyes, latency, page-load, timeouts and looked up ownership details in Backstage or scattered document like Links to their PagerDuty or track of all software (services, websites, libraries etc).
Even after all that effort, we often ended up scratching our heads, trying to stitch the story together under pressure, esp. when time is limited and expectation is high.
Today, that struggle looks very different.
 Our response to what we want to investigate is now just a question away.
 We let MCP-powered AI do the heavy lifting - reducing human effort, cutting noise, and giving us the context we need, when we need it..
When something went wrong in WebEx:
One engineer checked dashboards
Another searched logs
Someone else looked at network alerts
Ownership details lived in documents, wikis, or people's heads

As an Observability Engineer and Incident Commander, my responsibility was simple but stressful:
Ask the right questions
Find the right owners
Identify the real root cause
Restore services as fast as possible

The problem was not lack of tools - 
 the problem was too many tools and too much context switching.
During a major incident, even 5–10 minutes of delay feels like an eternity
That's when we started asking ourselves a serious question:
💡 What if AI could bring all this context to us - instantly at one single IDE?
🤖 Introducing AI into WebEx (With Purpose)
We were not interested in AI as a buzzword.
We wanted AI that could:
Understand our systems
Talk to our enterprise tools
Assist during live incidents
Reduce human fatigue
Speed up Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

This is where Model Context Protocol (MCP) became a turning point for us.
🔗 MCP in Simple Terms
Now what is MCP? Well think of MCP as a secure bridge 🌉 between:
Your AI assistant
Your real enterprise systems

Instead of AI only helping with code, it can now:
Query your software catalog
Check network alerts
Pull observability signals
Answer context-aware operational questions

In short:
🧠 AI stopped guessing - and started knowing.
🛠️ Our First Two AI Integrations
We started small but focused on maximum impact.
1️⃣ Backstage MCP - Instant Ownership & Context
Backstage is our internal source of truth:
Services
APIs
Teams
Ownership
Documentation

With Backstage connected to AI, I could simply ask:
"Who owns this service?"
"What depends on this component?"
"Show documentation for this API"

No more manual hunting through wikis 📚
No more guessing ownership during incidents
This alone saved precious minutes during outages.
2️⃣ ThousandEyes MCP - Clear Network Visibility 🌐
Network issues are often the hardest to debug:
ISP problems?
Cloud provider outage?
DNS?
SaaS dependency?

With ThousandEyes connected through MCP, AI could now:
List active alerts 🚨
Show affected tests
Identify outages
Highlight impacted regions or providers

Instead of logging into multiple portals, I just asked:
"What is alerting right now?"
"Are there any cloud or ISP outages?"
"Which tests are impacted?"

And the answers came inside the IDE.
That was a big shift.
